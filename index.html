Introduction

About Inconclusive Evidence

This web application is designed for the article by Riesthuis et al. (2026) titled “Decisions under Uncertainty: A Statistical Framework for Evaluating Practical Relevance in Interval-Based Hypothesis Testing”. The framework focuses on the possible outcomes when researchers set a Smallest Effect Size of Interest (SESOI) (or a minimally meaningful effect size, MMES) and conduct interval-based hypothesis tests. Specifically, it addresses the potential outcome wherein the practical relevance of an observed effect (e.g., mean difference) is inconclusive.

What is a SESOI? The SESOI defines the numerical threshold between effects that are practically meaningful and those that are trivial or negligible. In an interval-based framework, researchers define a lower bound (SESOIlower) and an upper bound (SESOIupper). An effect is declared practically meaningful if its entire confidence interval falls outside the SESOI range, and practically equivalent if its entire confidence interval falls strictly within the SESOI range.

Illustrative Example (SESOI set to [-5, 5]): In the example shown below, the SESOI is defined as [-5, 5] (a lower bound of -5 and an upper bound of +5):

  • Inconclusive Outcome 1 (Point Estimate = 5.5, SD = 0.75): The observed mean difference is 5.5 with a 95% CI [4.0, 7.0] and a 90% CI [4.5, 6.5]. Although the point estimate is greater than the upper SESOI bound of 5, both confidence intervals overlap with 5, meaning we cannot definitively claim practical relevance at the 90% or 95% level.
  • Inconclusive Outcome 2 (Point Estimate = 4.5, SD = 0.75): The observed mean difference is 4.5 with a 95% CI [3.0, 6.0] and a 90% CI [3.5, 5.5]. Although the point estimate is inside the equivalence boundary of 5, the 90% CI [3.5, 5.5] crosses the upper SESOI bound of 5, meaning we cannot claim practical equivalence either.

Note that declaring practical significance requires the 95% CI to exclude the SESOI in a two-tailed test, or the 90% CI in a one-tailed test (while equivalence testing uses the 90% CI).

Application Purpose

The purpose of this application is to provide researchers with tools to quantify the associated errors and robustness of minimum-effect and equivalence test results, guiding decision-making in situations of uncertainty. This app allows you to provide an estimate and standard error (or sample size + SD) and receive intuitive interpretations. Please refer to Riesthuis et al. (2026) for a more detailed description of these concepts and methods.

Frequentist CI-Based Decisions & Sensitivity Guide

Show Instructions

How to evaluate practical relevance and test robustness under frequentist estimation:

  • 1. Enter Observed Estimate & Uncertainty: Input your sample effect size (e.g. mean difference) and provide either the Standard Error (SE) directly or the sample size (N) and standard deviation (SD).
  • 2. Choose Test Type & SESOI Boundaries: Select whether you are testing for practical significance (Minimum-Effect Test) or practical equivalence (Equivalence Test), specify hypothesis tail direction, and define your Smallest Effect Size of Interest (SESOI lower and upper bounds).
  • 3. Specify Custom Alpha & Replication Sample Size: Set your nominal Type I error rate (α, e.g. 0.050) and the sample size planned for a hypothetical replication study.
  • 4. Interpret Decisions & Robustness:
    • Threshold Alpha: The exact maximum Type I error rate required to conclude practical relevance or equivalence.
    • Robustness Index: The factor by which your standard error or sample size would need to change to alter the practical conclusion.
    • Replication Probability: The probability that a future replication study will successfully demonstrate practical relevance.

Bayesian Posterior & Interval Bayes Factor Guide

Show Instructions

How to evaluate Bayesian posterior probabilities and evidence strength:

  • 1. Enter Observed Data & Prior Distribution: Provide your observed sample effect size and uncertainty (SE or Sample Size + SD), together with your prior beliefs (Prior Mean and Prior SD).
  • 2. Set Test Type & SESOI Boundaries: Select whether you are testing for practical significance (Minimum-Effect Test) or practical equivalence (Equivalence Test), and specify your SESOI bounds.
  • 3. Observe Prior-to-Posterior Updating: The plot illustrates how your prior (dashed gray curve) combines with sample likelihood to yield the posterior distribution (solid blue curve), with the shaded area showing the probability mass inside or outside the SESOI.
  • 4. Interpret Posterior Metrics & Evidence:
    • Posterior Probability: The quantified posterior probability that the true effect size is practically meaningful or practically equivalent.
    • Interval Bayes Factor (BF10 / BF01): The relative evidence favoring the practical hypothesis over the alternative hypothesis based on the ratio of posterior to prior odds.

Meta-Analytic Sensitivity & Future Studies Guide

Show Instructions

How to use this tool to evaluate the impact of future studies:

  • 1. Enter Existing Evidence: Supply the effect sizes (y), standard errors (SE), and sample sizes (N) of already published studies in the table below.
  • 2. Set SESOI Boundaries: Define your Smallest Effect Size of Interest (e.g., -1.0 to 1.0) on the same metric as your effect sizes.
  • 3. Simulate Future Study(ies): Add one or more hypothetical future studies (e.g. "Future Replication") with your expected effect size and target precision (sample size/SE).
  • 4. Observe Impact on Conclusions: Click Run Meta-Analysis to inspect how adding your planned study narrows the pooled interval and whether it turns an inconclusive meta-analytic result into a practically meaningful or equivalent outcome.
